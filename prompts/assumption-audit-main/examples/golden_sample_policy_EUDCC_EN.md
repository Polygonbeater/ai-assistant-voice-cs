# Golden sample (EN) – Policy audit: EU Digital COVID Certificate (time guardrail + “process as justification”)

**Case (real)**: The EU Digital COVID Certificate framework was extended until 30 June 2023; it was also communicated that it could be repealed earlier if the health situation allowed.  
**Sources**: Council of the EU press releases + EUR‑Lex end-of-validity information.

---

## Crisis Assumption Audit (A – politics/public sector)

1) **CLASSIFY**: A (politics/public sector). Modules: CSH + ISO 31000.  
2) **RED FLAG**: Hidden assumption: “keeping the certificate framework” is presented as technical necessity even though it is a political choice about governing mobility under uncertainty.  
   **STATUS**: Mixed — Fact (extension + date), high confidence, Evidence Tier A; Hypothesis (behavioral/system effects), medium confidence, Evidence Tier B/C. Missing: unified data on where/how the certificate was required; stop rule: explicit benefit metric + public review.

3) **CORES+**
- **Authority**: EU legislation (EP/Council) + Member States (travel requirements).  
- **Profit**: travel sector and states (Hypothesis: standardization lowers transaction costs; Evidence Tier B).  
- **Metric**: cross-border travel continuity vs. health risk.  
- **Default**: the “ready framework” remains in place; the choice becomes “keep” rather than “end” (inertia).  
- **Costs**: citizens (admin burden, access inequalities); states (infrastructure/operations).  
- **Excluded alternative**: purely national rules without a shared framework / or full shutdown with no fallback mechanism.

4) **3 TRADE-OFFS**
- Preparedness over normalization of an exceptional regime; cost borne by legitimacy.  
- Standardization over plural approaches; cost borne by states with different conditions.  
- Control speed over data minimization; cost borne by privacy and administrative load.

5) **2 ALTERNATIVES**
- Option 1: “Dormant mode” — framework exists but stays off, triggered only by explicit thresholds. Risk: slower response.  
- Option 2: repeal the framework and keep only interoperability guidance without a certificate system. Risk: fragmentation.

6) **TIME (sunsetting)**
- A fixed end date (30 Jun 2023) acts as a guardrail.  
- Better: add a **continuous trigger**: “repeal early if certificates aren’t required for X months and benefit metric falls below threshold.”

7) **PRE-MORTEM**
- Tick-box: “travel only” in theory, but it spills into services.  
- Bypass: states introduce parallel documents outside the framework.  
- Malicious compliance: strict enforcement makes free movement practically impossible.

8) **3 INTERVENTIONS**
- Mandatory public review: how often the framework was used and with what effect.  
- Data minimization + clear controller roles (who sees what and why).  
- Pre-defined activation AND deactivation thresholds (symmetry).

9) **2 PUBLISH-READY SENTENCES**
- “This isn’t just ‘process’: it’s a choice whether an exceptional regime stays as default readiness.”  
- “Benefit by which metric—and at whose expense when ‘temporary’ extends by inertia?”
