# Golden sample (EN) – Algorithmic audit: Australia’s “Robodebt” (automated debt raising)

**Case (real)**: “Robodebts” were debts raised between July 2015 and November 2019 under the Income Compliance Program. They were raised by using **averaged** Australian Taxation Office (ATO) income information. Subsequent official reviews and the Royal Commission describe the scheme as unlawful/defective and harmful.  
**Frame**: CSH (legitimacy/boundaries) + ISO 31000 (risk) + ALTAI/NIST (governance, accountability, risk management).

---

## Crisis Assumption Audit (C – AI/algorithm / automation)

1) **CLASSIFY**: C (automated decision-making/algorithm). Modules: CSH + ISO 31000 + ALTAI + NIST AI RMF.  
2) **RED FLAG**: Hidden assumption: “averaged data is sufficient evidence of debt” is presented as fact even though it is a methodological choice that shifts burden of proof and error rates.  
   **STATUS**: Mixed — Fact (time window + averaged ATO income), high confidence, Evidence Tier A. Harm/illegality characterization: Evidence Tier A/B (official report + official statements). Missing: comparable error-rate metrics (false positives/negatives) over time; stop rule: publish error rates + independent audit + clear redress for wrongly affected people.

3) **CORES+**
- **Authority**: Services Australia / government (scheme design) + legal framework (what counts as “evidence”).  
- **Profit**: fiscal savings / compliance “performance” (Hypothesis: pressure for yield and speed; Evidence Tier B/C).  
- **Metric**: volume of detected “debts”, recovery speed, mismatch detection.  
- **Default**: the system raises debt by default; the citizen must disprove it (asymmetry).  
- **Costs**: citizens (time, stress, financial insecurity), state (redress, reputation, legal risk).  
- **Excluded alternative**: require primary evidence (payslips) before raising debt; or mandatory human review for high-impact cases.

4) **3 TRADE-OFFS**
- Speed and scale over accuracy; cost borne by vulnerable people.  
- Compliance “throughput” over legal certainty; cost borne by state legitimacy.  
- Automation over real accountability; cost borne by redress and trust.

5) **2 ALTERNATIVES**
- Option 1: raise debt only after primary evidence is obtained (slower, more accurate).  
- Option 2: automation as triage only + mandatory human review for consequential decisions (costlier, fairer).

6) **TIME (sunsetting)**
- Any automated debt-recovery regime should have a hard sunset (e.g., 12 months) and continue only if it proves: (a) low error rates, (b) accessible redress, (c) transparent metrics.

7) **PRE-MORTEM**
- Tick-box: “review” exists but is overloaded and rubber-stamps outputs.  
- Bypass: human reviewers lack the info needed to challenge the system.  
- Malicious compliance: the agency demands documents people can’t realistically supply, making defense impossible “by the book”.

8) **3 INTERVENTIONS**
- Mandatory publication of metrics: error rates, appeals, time-to-redress, group impacts.  
- Right to a human + fast redress (refund/apology/stop recovery during dispute).  
- Independent audit + clear accountability for method/model changes.

9) **2 PUBLISH-READY SENTENCES**
- “If an algorithm makes debt the default, the state must prove it—not the citizen.”  
- “Efficient by which metric—and at whose expense when the cost of error hits the weakest?”
